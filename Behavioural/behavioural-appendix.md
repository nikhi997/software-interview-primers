## A. The story-bank template

Copy this once per story. Aim for 8–12 filled out (Chapter 3), with 2–3 multi-signal "anchor" stories.

```
STORY TITLE: ________________________________   (a short handle)

SITUATION (1–2 sentences — the context):
  ____________________________________________________

TASK (1–2 sentences — YOUR specific charge / the stakes):
  ____________________________________________________

ACTIONS (the "I" list — the longest field, in order):
  - I ________________________________________________
  - I ________________________________________________
  - I ________________________________________________
  - (include the KEY DECISIONS and WHY you made them)

RESULT (the outcome — with a NUMBER, plus the lesson):
  - Impact / metric: __________________________________
  - What I learned:  __________________________________

SIGNALS THIS STORY DELIVERS (tag all that apply):
  [ ] Ownership/initiative  [ ] Impact  [ ] Conflict/collaboration
  [ ] Ambiguity  [ ] Growth/self-awareness  [ ] Communication

LIKELY FOLLOW-UPS (pre-answer these):
  - Why did you choose ___ over ___?  → ______________
  - What would you do differently?    → ______________
  - What was the hardest part?        → ______________
  - How did others react?             → ______________
```

> The **"what would you do differently?"** line is mandatory for every story — it's the most common follow-up and the clearest test of self-awareness (Chapter 3, Chapter 5).

## C. Question bank by family

Drill these: read one, decode its signal, name which story you'd play. (Chapters in parentheses.)

**Conflict & disagreement (Ch 4)**
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a coworker.
- A time you disagreed with your manager.
- A time you had to convince someone to do something your way.
- A time you received significant pushback on an idea.
- A time another team and yours were at odds.
- A time you disagreed but committed to the group's decision anyway.

**Failure, mistakes & feedback (Ch 5)**
- Tell me about your biggest failure.
- A time you made a significant mistake. What happened?
- A time you broke something in production.
- A project that didn't go as planned.
- The most critical feedback you've received, and what you did with it.
- A time you had to admit you were wrong.

**Leadership, ownership & initiative (Ch 6)**
- Tell me about a time you showed leadership.
- A time you took ownership of something outside your assigned role.
- A time you drove a project from start to finish.
- A time you influenced people without authority over them.
- A time you took initiative no one asked you to take.
- A time you improved a process or tool for your team.

**Ambiguity, prioritization & deadlines (Ch 7)**
- A time you worked with unclear or changing requirements.
- A time you had to make a decision without all the information.
- A time you juggled competing priorities. How did you choose?
- A time you were going to miss a deadline.
- A time you had to cut scope or say no.
- A time a project you were on was running late. How did the team respond — add people or cut scope? *(Bonus signal: naming Brooks's Law — adding people to a late project makes it later.)*
- A time you had to learn something quickly to get the job done.

**General / opener**
- Tell me about yourself. *(A 60–90s narrative: who you are, a highlight or two, why this role.)*
- Walk me through a project you're proud of.
- Why this company / this role?
- Where do you want to grow next?

## D. The signal map

The core lookup: question → what it's scoring → which kind of story to reach for.

| Question type | What it's really scoring | Reach for a story showing... |
|---|---|---|
| Disagreement / conflict | Do you engage other views, resolve maturely? | You steelmanning the other side, resolving with data/a test, owning when wrong |
| Convince / influence | Influence without authority | You winning a skeptic via prototype/data, not position |
| Biggest failure / mistake | Self-awareness + growth | A real owned mistake → recovery → lesson with teeth |
| Critical feedback | Can you hear hard truths and change? | Hard feedback → honest reaction → concrete change |
| Leadership / led a project | Ownership + drive + follow-through | You driving an unowned outcome to done |
| Initiative / went beyond role | Bias for action, "I noticed, so I" | You acting before being asked, quantified result |
| Unclear requirements / ambiguity | Confident progress without full info | Explicit assumptions, cheap uncertainty reduction, iteration |
| Competing priorities | Impact-based judgment | You prioritizing by impact + aligning stakeholders |
| Tight deadline / slipping | Communication under pressure | Early flag + options, not heroics or a silent miss |
| Proudest project | Impact + communication | Your biggest quantified win, told cleanly |

## E. Red-flags self-audit checklist

Before any behavioural round, check your stories and delivery against the patterns interviewers screen *out* (Chapter 9):

- [ ] **No badmouthing** — every former employer/manager/teammate described factually and neutrally, then pivot to my actions.
- [ ] **No blame deflection** — in every failure story, I own my specific part with "I," not "they/the requirements/circumstances."
- [ ] **Real weakness/failure** — I have a genuine, owned failure (not "perfectionism," not a humblebrag).
- [ ] **Specifics, not buzzwords** — I can go deep on every story; numbers and concrete actions, not vague claims.
- [ ] **"I" not "we"** — every story makes my individual contribution unmistakable while crediting others.
- [ ] **A number per story** — each story lands a quantified or concretely-evidenced impact.
- [ ] **Flexibility shown** — at least one conflict story where I lost/adjusted gracefully, not always "I was right."
- [ ] **Honesty** — every detail is true and survives "why?" asked twice.
- [ ] **Calm ownership tone** — confident about my actions, generous with credit, honest about limits; no arrogance, no false modesty.
- [ ] **Fluent not scripted** — I know each story's beats but speak them fresh, not as a memorized monologue.
- [ ] **Length matched** — stories land in 2–3 minutes; I match answer length to the question's scope.
- [ ] **Coverage complete** — every signal column in my matrix has ≥2 stories.

## F. The five-second decision flow (in the room)

When a question lands and the pressure spikes, run this (Chapter 10):

1. **Decode** — what signal is this *really* asking for?
2. **Pick the card** — which bank story is tagged for that signal? (Pause; "let me think of a good example" is fine.)
3. **STAR it** — brief S/T, detailed A in "I", R with a number + lesson.
4. **Welcome the dig** — answer follow-ups openly; this confirms it's real.

> *Every behavioural question is reading a hidden signal. Stop telling the story you want to tell — tell the one that delivers the signal they're scoring, with your actions and your numbers at the center.*
