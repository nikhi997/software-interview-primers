# Behavioural Mock Deck: cards, follow-ups, and scoring anchors

*[← Chapter 10](chapter-10/behavioural-chapter-10.md) · [Contents](behavioural-README.md)*

The chapters teach. The appendix looks up. The signal router decides under pressure. This deck rehearses the whole loop: cold prompt, signal decode, STAR answer, follow-up dig, score, debrief, next drill.

Everything here is generic. Keep your personalized story bank in a private or ignored file. Anonymize employers, systems, customers, and people; respect NDA boundaries; never invent numbers; never claim a team outcome as yours alone.

---

## How to run a 45-minute mock

1. The interviewer picks 5–7 cards without telling the candidate the target signal.
2. The candidate answers each prompt in 2–3 minutes, using a true private story.
3. The interviewer asks two or three follow-ups from the card's tree.
4. Score only the **target signal or signals** plus the baseline **Communication / STAR** dimension.
5. Mark untested signals **N/O** for that answer. Track six-signal coverage across the **session**, not inside every answer.
6. Debrief after each card for one minute, then pick the next drill.

> 💡 **Concept notes — don't over-score one answer**
> A conflict answer should not be graded on every signal in the book. It should be graded on conflict, maybe a secondary signal if the card names one, and whether the answer was clear. Untested signals are **N/O**. Coverage is a session-level question: after the mock, did the set of questions test ownership, impact, collaboration, ambiguity, growth, and communication?

## Baseline Communication / STAR scorecard

Use this on every card.

| Score | Observable anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Answer lacks a clear Situation, Task, Action, or Result; interviewer cannot identify what the candidate personally did. |
| 2 | STAR parts are present but uneven; setup runs long, actions are partly "we," or the result lacks outcome evidence. |
| 3 | Brief setup, clear personal actions, result or concrete evidence, and a lesson; answer lands in roughly 2–3 minutes. |
| 4 | Same as 3, plus the answer adapts to the exact prompt, signposts tradeoffs, and handles follow-up without defensiveness or recitation. |

## Card 1 — The opener

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about yourself."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Communication.
**Secondary signal:** Motivation.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- gives a concise professional arc instead of a full biography;
- names one or two proof points relevant to the role;
- connects their direction to the opportunity without generic flattery;
- leaves room for the interviewer to choose a follow-up.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "Which part of that path did you actively choose rather than drift into?"
- **Judgment:** "Why is this next step the right one for you now?"
- **Evidence:** "What's one result that best proves that direction?"
- **Collaboration:** "Who helped shape that path, and how did you use their input?"
- **Reflection:** "What would you change about how you made those choices?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why does that matter to you? Why that, specifically?"

### Red flags to listen for

- chronological life story with no signal;
- generic claims that fit any role;
- over-rehearsed monologue;
- inflated claims with no evidence;
- no clear reason for the current role.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Communication | No clear arc; answer exceeds scope or misses the role. | Arc exists but is resume-like or generic. | 60–90 second arc with relevant proof points and role connection. | Arc is concise, specific, conversational, and invites a useful follow-up. |
| Motivation | Reason is absent or purely external. | Reason could apply unchanged to almost any role. | Reason connects the candidate's direction to the role's work. | Reason includes tradeoff: what they are choosing this role for and what they are deliberately leaving behind. |
| Untested signals | Ownership N/O; Impact N/O; Collaboration N/O; Ambiguity N/O; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What three words should the interviewer remember about you after this answer?" Next drill: cut the answer to 75 seconds without losing the proof point.

## Card 2 — Proudest project

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Walk me through a project you're proud of."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Impact.
**Secondary signal:** Ownership.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- explains why the project mattered before explaining how it worked;
- separates their actions from the team's actions;
- gives a true metric, honest estimate, or concrete before/after;
- explains why this is the story they chose for pride.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What did you personally do that would not have happened otherwise?"
- **Judgment:** "What tradeoff did you make, and what alternative did you reject?"
- **Evidence:** "What was the baseline, and how did you know the result improved?"
- **Collaboration:** "How did others react to your approach?"
- **Reflection:** "What would you do differently if you repeated it?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why are you proud of that part, not just the final outcome?"

### Red flags to listen for

- project sounds impressive but the candidate's role is foggy;
- no impact evidence;
- technical tour replaces behavioural signal;
- candidate overclaims team work;
- pride is based on complexity, not value delivered.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Impact | No outcome or only "it was better." | Outcome named but baseline, scope, or evidence is unclear. | Before/after, metric, estimate, scope, or risk reduction is stated truthfully. | Evidence is tied to why the work mattered and what decision it changed or enabled. |
| Ownership | Candidate says mostly "we" and personal contribution is unclear. | Personal contribution appears in one or two moments. | Candidate names the decisions and actions they personally owned. | Candidate owns their work clearly while crediting teammates and naming dependencies. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Collaboration N/O unless follow-up probes it; Ambiguity N/O; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "If the interviewer wrote one evidence sentence, what would it say?" Next drill: retell the same project as an ownership answer, then as an impact answer, changing the emphasis.

## Card 3 — Disagreement with a teammate

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate on an important decision."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Collaboration / conflict.
**Secondary signal:** Communication.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- states the other person's view fairly before arguing their own;
- keeps the disagreement about the work, not personalities;
- uses a resolution mechanism such as data, a test, a trial, or escalation with options;
- can name what they learned or adjusted.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What did you do to move the disagreement toward resolution?"
- **Judgment:** "Why was your proposed path better, and where might it have been worse?"
- **Evidence:** "What data, prototype, or user signal helped settle it?"
- **Collaboration:** "How would the other person describe your behavior in that disagreement?"
- **Reflection:** "What would you change about how you handled it?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why did this matter to you? Why not just let it go?"

### Red flags to listen for

- other person described as foolish, lazy, or political;
- candidate's goal is winning;
- no resolution mechanism;
- defensiveness when asked about the other side;
- story ends with resentment or victory lap.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Collaboration / conflict | Candidate blames, mocks, or skips the other view. | Other view is mentioned but not fairly explained. | Candidate steelmans the other view and shows a constructive resolution. | Candidate also shows how the relationship or decision quality improved, including what they adjusted. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Ownership N/O unless follow-up probes action depth; Impact N/O; Ambiguity N/O; Growth N/O unless reflection is probed |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "Could the other person recognize themselves in that version?" Next drill: retell the first 45 seconds with the other side stated before your side.

## Card 4 — Influence without authority

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a time you had to influence people without having authority over them."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Ownership / drive.
**Secondary signal:** Collaboration / conflict.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- names the unowned outcome they decided to drive;
- explains why people were not already aligned;
- shows the influence mechanism: data, prototype, trust, coalition, or making the path easier;
- follows through after agreement.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What did you own end to end?"
- **Judgment:** "Why did you choose that influence approach instead of another one?"
- **Evidence:** "What convinced the first skeptic?"
- **Collaboration:** "Who resisted, and how did you handle their concern?"
- **Reflection:** "What would you do earlier next time?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why did you care enough to push this without authority?"

### Red flags to listen for

- authority or title is the real mechanism;
- "I convinced everyone" with no details;
- no resistance;
- no follow-through;
- candidate takes sole credit for a coalition outcome.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership / drive | Candidate was assigned the task or only suggested an idea. | Candidate took action but did not drive to a result. | Candidate noticed a problem, took responsibility, influenced others, and landed an outcome. | Candidate also shows how the change stayed adopted after the initial push. |
| Collaboration / conflict | Resistance is absent or dismissed. | Resistance is named but not engaged. | Candidate addresses concerns directly and brings others along. | Candidate turns resistance into a better plan and credits others visibly. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Impact N/O unless measured; Ambiguity N/O; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What was the influence mechanism in one phrase?" Next drill: answer again with that mechanism stated explicitly in the Action section.

## Card 5 — Significant mistake

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a significant mistake you made."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Growth / self-awareness.
**Secondary signal:** Ownership.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- chooses a real but safe mistake;
- owns their part without blaming the environment;
- describes immediate recovery;
- names a concrete behavior that changed afterward.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What was specifically yours to own?"
- **Judgment:** "What decision would you make differently now?"
- **Evidence:** "How do you know the lesson stuck?"
- **Collaboration:** "How did you communicate with people affected by it?"
- **Reflection:** "What did this change about how you work?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why was this hard to admit? Why this example?"

### Red flags to listen for

- fake failure;
- blame deflection;
- catastrophic ethics or judgment issue;
- lesson is "be more careful" with no behavior;
- no recovery action.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth / self-awareness | Candidate denies fault, gives a fake mistake, or offers no lesson. | Candidate owns some fault but lesson is vague. | Candidate owns a real mistake, recovers, and names a concrete changed behavior. | Candidate gives evidence the changed behavior prevented recurrence or improved later work. |
| Ownership | Responsibility is shifted to others or circumstances. | Candidate owns a narrow slice but keeps explaining external causes. | Candidate states their part plainly and describes what they did next. | Candidate owns their part without over-owning what belonged to others, and communicates transparently. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Impact N/O unless consequence/recovery is measured; Collaboration N/O unless communication is probed; Ambiguity N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What is the changed behavior, in one sentence?" Next drill: replace any vague lesson with a specific practice, checklist, review habit, escalation habit, or test you actually use.

## Card 6 — Critical feedback

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about critical feedback you received and what you did with it."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Growth / self-awareness.
**Secondary signal:** Collaboration / conflict.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- chooses feedback that was genuinely hard to hear;
- admits their initial reaction without defensiveness;
- changes a visible behavior;
- shows the relationship or work improved afterward.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What part of the feedback was fair?"
- **Judgment:** "How did you decide what to change first?"
- **Evidence:** "What did people notice after you changed?"
- **Collaboration:** "How did you follow up with the person who gave the feedback?"
- **Reflection:** "What do you still watch for now?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why did that feedback sting? Why was it worth acting on?"

### Red flags to listen for

- feedback is actually praise;
- candidate argues the feedback was wrong;
- no behavior changed;
- no follow-up with the giver;
- answer becomes a complaint about the person who delivered it.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth / self-awareness | Candidate cannot name real critical feedback or rejects it. | Candidate accepts feedback in words but change is unclear. | Candidate names feedback, reaction, action taken, and observed improvement. | Candidate also shows an ongoing self-check that keeps the behavior from returning. |
| Collaboration / conflict | Feedback giver is blamed or dismissed. | Candidate stays neutral but does not engage the relationship. | Candidate follows up and uses the feedback to improve collaboration. | Candidate turns the feedback loop into a stronger working relationship or team norm. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Ownership N/O unless probed; Impact N/O; Ambiguity N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What changed that another person could observe?" Next drill: answer the same prompt in 90 seconds, spending most time on the change, not the criticism.

## Card 7 — Leadership without a title

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a time you showed leadership without being the formal lead."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Ownership / drive.
**Secondary signal:** Impact.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- identifies an unowned problem;
- takes responsibility without waiting for permission;
- coordinates or influences others;
- drives the outcome to completion with evidence it mattered.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What would have happened if you had not stepped in?"
- **Judgment:** "How did you decide this was worth owning?"
- **Evidence:** "What changed after your leadership?"
- **Collaboration:** "How did you bring others along without making it about you?"
- **Reflection:** "Where did you overstep or under-communicate?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why did you take this on when it wasn't formally yours?"

### Red flags to listen for

- candidate equates leadership with status;
- no unowned problem;
- no result;
- coordination work is invisible;
- credit is not shared.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership / drive | Candidate had a title or assigned task but shows no extra ownership. | Candidate helped but did not drive the outcome. | Candidate noticed, owned, coordinated, and landed an unowned outcome. | Candidate also built a repeatable mechanism so the outcome did not depend on heroics. |
| Impact | No evidence the leadership mattered. | Outcome is stated but not evidenced. | Candidate gives a true metric, estimate, before/after, scope, or unblock. | Candidate connects the impact to why the team or project changed course. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Collaboration N/O unless follow-up probes it; Ambiguity N/O; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What was the unowned pain?" Next drill: start the answer with that pain in one sentence, then show why you owned it.

## Card 8 — Initiative or process improvement

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a time you improved a process, tool, or way of working without being asked."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Ownership / drive.
**Secondary signal:** Impact.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- uses the "I noticed X, so I did Y" spine;
- explains why the old process hurt;
- ships a practical improvement, not just a proposal;
- measures or concretely describes the before/after.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What did you do before anyone asked?"
- **Judgment:** "Why did you choose this fix over a larger or smaller one?"
- **Evidence:** "What was the before/after?"
- **Collaboration:** "Who had to adopt it, and how did you make that happen?"
- **Reflection:** "What did you learn about changing team habits?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why did this bother you enough to act?"

### Red flags to listen for

- improvement was assigned;
- solution was never adopted;
- impact is convenience-only and vague;
- candidate ignores adoption and change management;
- answer overstates a small contribution.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ownership / drive | Candidate only noticed or complained about the problem. | Candidate proposed a fix but adoption or completion is unclear. | Candidate acted without being asked and got the improvement used. | Candidate also followed up to keep the improvement alive or teach others. |
| Impact | No before/after. | Before/after is asserted but not concrete. | Candidate states time saved, errors reduced, people unblocked, risk reduced, or adoption scope. | Candidate ties the improvement to a team-level outcome beyond personal convenience. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Collaboration N/O unless adoption is probed; Ambiguity N/O; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What pain did the old way create?" Next drill: retell with the old pain and the adoption mechanism made explicit.

## Card 9 — Unclear requirements

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a time you had to make progress with unclear or changing requirements."

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Ambiguity.
**Secondary signal:** Communication.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- names what was unknown;
- reduces uncertainty cheaply;
- states assumptions visibly;
- starts with a small learning step;
- adjusts as new information arrives.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "What did you do instead of waiting for clarity?"
- **Judgment:** "Which assumption was riskiest, and how did you test it?"
- **Evidence:** "What signal told you to continue or pivot?"
- **Collaboration:** "Who needed to agree with your assumptions?"
- **Reflection:** "What would you clarify earlier next time?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why was this ambiguous? Why couldn't you just ask for a full spec?"

### Red flags to listen for

- candidate waited passively;
- unknowns are not named;
- assumptions were hidden;
- action was reckless rather than reversible;
- no stakeholder communication.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ambiguity | Candidate needed complete clarity before acting or acted blindly. | Candidate moved, but assumptions or learning steps are unclear. | Candidate named unknowns, made assumptions visible, reduced risk, and iterated. | Candidate also shows how the ambiguity handling improved alignment or avoided rework. |
| Communication | Stakeholders were surprised or absent. | Candidate communicated status but not tradeoffs or assumptions. | Candidate aligned people on assumptions, progress, and changes. | Candidate gave stakeholders options and reset expectations before surprises became misses. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard too; if duplicative, record one communication score and note why. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Ownership N/O unless probed; Impact N/O unless outcome is measured; Collaboration N/O; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What was the assumption you made out loud?" Next drill: retell the Action section as unknown → assumption → cheap test → adjustment.

## Card 10 — Priorities or deadline pressure

### Candidate-facing prompt

"Tell me about a time you had too many priorities or a deadline was at risk. How did you handle it?"

### Interviewer-only target signal + what evidence counts

**Target signal:** Ambiguity / prioritization.
**Secondary signal:** Communication under pressure.

Evidence counts when the candidate:

- explains the competing demands or deadline risk early;
- chooses based on impact, risk, blockers, effort, or deadline value;
- communicates tradeoffs before stakeholders are surprised;
- offers options such as cut scope, shift date, sequence work, or split independent work.

### Follow-up tree

- **Ownership:** "When did you realize the plan was at risk, and what did you do first?"
- **Judgment:** "What did you choose not to do, and why?"
- **Evidence:** "What data or constraint made that priority order clear?"
- **Collaboration:** "Who had to agree to the tradeoff?"
- **Reflection:** "What signal would you watch earlier next time?"
- **Authenticity:** "Why not just work longer? Why was that the wrong or insufficient answer?"

### Red flags to listen for

- silent miss;
- heroics are the whole solution;
- no prioritization basis;
- stakeholder learns late;
- candidate claims everything was done without tradeoff.

### Per-answer scorecard

| Dimension | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ambiguity / prioritization | Candidate did everything, froze, or chose by ease/loudest voice. | Candidate prioritized but did not connect the choice to impact, risk, blockers, effort, or deadline value. | Candidate chooses using impact, risk, blockers, effort, or deadline value and explains the tradeoff. | Candidate also shows how the choice protected the most important outcome while making the cost explicit. |
| Communication under pressure | Candidate surprised people or hid the risk. | Candidate reported the risk late or without options. | Candidate raised risk early with options and recommendation. | Candidate aligned stakeholders on a changed plan and preserved trust through updates. |
| Communication / STAR | Use the baseline scorecard; if the pressure communication score is separate, keep STAR focused on structure. |  |  |  |
| Untested signals | Ownership N/O unless probed; Impact N/O unless outcome is measured; Collaboration N/O unless alignment is probed; Growth N/O |  |  |  |

### Debrief + next drill

Ask: "What option did you put on the table?" Next drill: answer again with exactly three options and your recommendation stated before the result.

---

## The bumper sticker

> *A mock is not a performance review of your personality. It is a signal drill: target signal, observable evidence, follow-up depth, STAR clarity, then session coverage. Score what was tested, mark the rest N/O, and drill the weakest signal next.*
