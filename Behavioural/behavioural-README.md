# Behavioural Primer — Reading order and study contract

A 10-chapter primer on the behavioural interview for software engineers, written in the conversational style of the LLD, HLD, and DSA primers in this collection. Built around a single principle: *answer the question behind the question.*

The other three primers train what you can *build*. This one trains whether a team will *want to build it with you* — and that round decides more offers, levels, and rejections than most engineers realize.

## The one idea

Most engineers treat behavioural rounds as a soft formality — "just tell them about a project" — and wing it. Then they get dinged for "not enough leadership signal" or "couldn't go deep on impact," and they have no idea why, because the questions sounded easy.

Here's the thing: behavioural questions are never about the surface story. "Tell me about a conflict" is not asking *what the conflict was* — it's probing *how you handle people who disagree with you, whether you can see other viewpoints, and whether you escalate or collaborate.* Every question has a **hidden signal** it's trying to read.

We're going to make those signals visible. For every question type, you'll learn what the interviewer is *actually* scoring, then how to structure a story that delivers exactly that signal — concrete, specific, and centered on *your* actions and *measurable* impact. You won't memorize scripts. You'll build a **story bank** and learn to aim the right story at the signal behind any question.

## How behavioural interviews are actually scored

Interviewers are usually filling out a rubric with named dimensions — the exact names vary by company (Amazon's Leadership Principles, Google's "Googleyness," generic "ownership / collaboration / impact") but they cluster into a handful of signals:

1. **Ownership / drive** — do you take responsibility beyond your assignment, push through obstacles?
2. **Impact** — did your work *matter*, and can you quantify it?
3. **Collaboration / conflict** — do you work well with others, handle disagreement maturely?
4. **Dealing with ambiguity** — can you make progress without complete information?
5. **Growth / self-awareness** — do you learn from failure, seek and act on feedback?
6. **Communication** — can you tell a clear, structured, honest story?

This book builds a story bank that hits all six, and teaches you to recognize which one any question is fishing for.

## Reading order

Read in sequence — the early chapters build the toolkit the later ones apply.

**Part 1 — The toolkit**
- Chapter 1: The question behind the question *(what's really being assessed)*
- Chapter 2: STAR, and how to not ramble *(structuring any answer)*
- Chapter 3: Building your story bank *(mining your experience for material)*

**Part 2 — The question families**
- Chapter 4: Conflict and disagreement *(the most common, most fumbled family)*
- Chapter 5: Failure, mistakes, and feedback *(showing growth without self-destructing)*
- Chapter 6: Leadership, ownership, and initiative *(driving without authority)*
- Chapter 7: Ambiguity, prioritization, and deadlines *(progress under uncertainty)*

**Part 3 — Delivery and the room**
- Chapter 8: The questions *you* ask *(turning the tables)*
- Chapter 9: Red flags, tells, and delivery *(how to not sink a good story)*
- Chapter 10: The mock and the ritual *(putting it together under pressure)*

**Appendix:** The story-bank template, a question bank by family, the signal map (question → what it's scoring → which story to use), and a red-flags checklist.

## Companion tools

The chapters teach the skill, the appendix gives you lookup tables, and these two companion tools help you use the skill under pressure:

- [Signal router](behavioural-signal-router.md) — use from Chapter 1 onward whenever you practice decoding a prompt into the probable signal and choosing the right story.
- [Mock deck](behavioural-mock-deck.md) — use from Chapter 10, or any time you are rehearsing out loud with follow-ups and scorecards.

## Prerequisites

- At least one project, internship, or job you can talk about in detail. (Students: course projects, hackathons, open source, and club leadership all count.)
- Honesty. Fabricated stories collapse under follow-up questions, and interviewers are trained to dig.

## Study contract — do not skip

Behavioural prep fails when treated as reading. It's **writing and rehearsal.** Each chapter is **3 sessions, NOT one sitting:**

**Session 1 (45 min):** Read the chapter. As you read, jot down which of *your* experiences fit the question family it covers — raw notes, not polished stories.

**Session 2 (45–60 min, next day):** Turn 2–3 of those notes into full written stories using the story-bank template (appendix). Write them out — bullet form is fine, but write the *numbers* and *your specific actions* down. Vague stories are the #1 failure mode; writing forces specificity.

**Session 3 (30 min, day after):** Say each story **out loud**, on a timer (aim for 2–3 minutes each). Record yourself or tell a friend. Then have someone ask one follow-up ("why did you do it that way?", "what would you do differently?") — the follow-ups are where interviews are won or lost.

≈2.5 hours per chapter, and the payoff compounds: most stories serve several question families, so by the end you'll have a reusable bank of 8–12 stories that answer almost anything.

## Checkpoints

- **After Ch3:** Do you have at least 6 written stories in the template, each with quantified impact and a clear "I did X" spine?
- **After Ch7:** For each question family (conflict, failure, leadership, ambiguity), can you name *which* of your stories you'd reach for, and why?
- **After Ch10:** Can you take a cold behavioural question, pick a story in 5 seconds, and deliver it in STAR form in under 3 minutes — out loud, without rambling?

If a checkpoint fails, go back and write more stories. The bank is the whole game.

## The bumper sticker

> *Every behavioural question is reading a hidden signal. Stop telling the story you want to tell — tell the one that delivers the signal they're scoring, with your actions and your numbers at the center.*
